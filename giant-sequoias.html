AI for Giant Sequoias

Satellite AI for monitoring every giant sequoia, one tree at a time
Contact Us
Dead giant sequoias among living ones. Photo: N. Stephenson, USGS
Giant sequoias have stood for thousands of years, and some of the most iconic trees are now dying.

AI for Giant Sequoias develops AI tools that automate forest survey and monitoring. We combine high-resolution satellite imagery with linked ground observations to bring individual tree monitoring to the landscape scale. Wildbook helps researchers follow individual animals, and this project applies the same idea to individual trees.

The Threat

Tiny native bark beetles are now killing millennia-old, iconic individual trees in as little as 18 months. Spotting stressed trees early, across whole groves, is the key to protecting them.
A native bark beetle (Phloeosinus). Photo: Joshua Dunlap, Oregon Department of Agriculture
Bark beetle galleries. Photo: Nick Ampersee

Our Goals

1. Count and inventory every giant sequoia.
Map each tree from space so every sequoia has a record.

2. Count and inventory every dead and dying tree.
Track losses from beetles and fire across whole groves.

3. Monitor tree health continuously.
Build an ongoing health monitoring system that flags trees potentially threatened by beetles at the earliest possible stage.
Hand-labeled tree crowns used to train our models

Dataset
• 8 groves labeled: Redwood Mountain, Giant Forest, Redwood Meadow, Muir, Oriole Lake, Lost, Garfield, and Cedar Flat
• More than 40,000 hand labels across 7 classes: tree, mature sequoia, spiretop sequoia, top-down threat, fire damage, dead, and healthy
• Diverse conditions: topography, seasonality, sun angle, and sensor geometry
Models and tools
• A general tree crown detection model for 0.3 m Pléiades Neo satellite imagery
• Two crown classifiers, one for species and one for health
• A QGIS plugin for end-to-end inference
• Independent species and health probabilities across high-concentration sequoia groves
Top-down threat cases: crowns declining from the top, flagged in satellite imagery (arrows)

AI for Individual Trees

What if every tree could be a “sensor” for invasive species, diseases, investment risk, and fire?

The problems we are working on
• Ground-based sampling for pests and pathogens is limited and disconnected
• High-resolution forest health monitoring is missing across large landscapes
• Coordination is lacking across public, private, industrial, and tribal lands
• Remote sensing is still too expensive

Our approach
We link diverse industries, researchers, resource managers, and data types. That supports multimodal observation for predicting high-severity fire, assessing risk, and detecting pests and pathogens early.

A “CARFAX” for Trees

Every car has a history that many industries rely on. We think individual trees can work the same way.
Industries linked around individual cars
• Departments of motor vehicles
• Manufacturers, auctions, and dealers
• Insurance companies
• Service and repair facilities
• Law enforcement and fire departments
Industries linked around individual trees
• Cities and urban forestry
• County, state, and federal resource managers
• Commercial forestry
• Insurance
• Individual property owners
• Arborists
• Nonprofits
The goal is opt-in, multimodal business intelligence about individual trees, and about the benefits and risks they bring, for all of these groups.

What We Are Doing Now

We are building a user interface for targeted survey workflows. The prototype has confidence sliders for species and health, a queue of anomalies ranked by probability, and a panel for reviewing the satellite imagery.
